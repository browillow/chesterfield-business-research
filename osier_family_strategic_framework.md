## 1. Purpose and use

This document preserves the purpose, context, research agenda, and decision principles for developing a durable Osier family enterprise rooted in Chesterfield County, Virginia. It is intended to let a new human or AI collaborator begin useful work without the original conversation.

The long-term ambition is an enduring family institution with productive ownership, dependable capabilities, relationships, and the ability to develop people and support worthwhile community institutions. The next three years are a foundation-building period within that generational ambition.

This is a strategic framework, not a selected business plan or a commitment to a particular corporate structure, investment, technology forecast, or career transition.

## 2. Founder context

Jordan lives in Chesterfield County and is the primary family breadwinner. He has approximately eight years of cloud-hosted, full-stack web application development and three years of internal enterprise product management experience. He is a full-time Principal Associate Software Engineer at Capital One, leading a small team recently redeployed to improve developer experience for integrating payment rails with the enterprise payments platform.

He is married, has three children, and is expecting a fourth child in March 2027. Household compensation, essential spending, liquid reserves, debts, available business hours, and his wife's desired participation have not been established in this discussion. Do not invent these constraints or presume unpaid family labor.

Jordan is a worship leader at LIFE CO, a nondenominational church in Chester, Virginia, and is teaching a ten-week systematic theology class. He co-founded TheoTank, a sermon-preparation SaaS product, with his lead and executive pastors. He reports little traction and is considering closing it. An asynchronous choir setlist and shared vocal-guide application is another idea under consideration. Neither project has been selected as the foundation of this strategy; shutdown of TheoTank has not been decided.

Jordan is open to traditional and nontraditional business models, partnerships, ownership, and changes in the kind of work he performs. Technical skills are an asset to apply across possible directions, rather than a requirement that the eventual enterprise sell software.

## 3. Stated purpose and desired inheritance

Jordan wants the family to have helped Chesterfield remain an economically, culturally, and spiritually healthy and viable place to live through the emergence of extremely capable AI. He wants the family venture or institution to remain a safe haven for his children while preserving their freedom to pursue something else.

He also wants a recognizable local reputation: when a particular important responsibility needs to be handled, people and business owners trust the Osiers to take care of it.

Working synthesis:

> Build an Osier family institution that helps Chesterfield adapt and flourish through technological change, earns durable resources and ownership through useful contribution, and gives future generations a dependable home base with freedom to choose their own paths.

The “Medici” metaphor expresses the ambition to combine enterprise, ownership, talent development, cultural contribution, and continuity. It does not specify a banking business, political program, or literal historical model.

## 4. What is established, provisional, and unresolved

### Established direction

- Family security and freedom matter alongside community contribution.
- The geographic anchor is Chesterfield County, with relevant regional and national connections.
- Economic, cultural, and spiritual health are all part of the purpose.
- The research should be ambitious and systematic, using AI agents to extend its breadth.
- Publicly available data is the initial priority for the county model.
- The family is open to different industries and institutional forms.

### Working proposals, subject to revision

- Preserve the benefits of engineering employment while testing ownership opportunities.
- Use paid, bounded experiments to establish commercial demand and delivery economics.
- Develop one dependable operating capability before expanding organizational complexity.
- Build a county observatory as a strategic research asset; test paid uses separately.
- Aim for capabilities and assets that other people can operate and steward.

### Open decisions

- Household risk capacity and sustainable time allocation, especially around the birth.
- Relative priority of family independence, active business leadership, ownership, teaching, and patronage.
- Whether the initial vehicle is an operating company, professional service, partnership, acquisition, software product, or another arrangement.
- The commercial future of TheoTank and the choir application idea.
- The desired roles of the spouse, extended family, nonfamily partners, and eventual successors.
- Whether and how the county model should be commercialized.

## 5. Planning stance toward AI and uncertainty

Jordan considers major disruption of knowledge work within roughly three years plausible and important enough to prepare for. Use that concern as a strategic motivation. Treat the timing, form, and consequences of ASI as uncertain.

Research several futures:

| Scenario | Strategic question |
| --- | --- |
| Broad augmentation with uneven adoption | How can the family and local organizations become more capable? |
| Rapid compression of knowledge-work staffing and prices | Which income, ownership, and service arrangements remain useful? |
| Concentration of capabilities and economic returns | Where can local organizations retain agency and access to productive resources? |
| Disruption combined with an economic downturn | Which commitments remain sustainable under reduced demand and income? |
| Slower or materially different technological change | Does the chosen enterprise still solve a worthwhile problem? |

Avoid treating task exposure as a forecast of job loss or treating cheaper production as guaranteed business profit. Customers, competitors, platform vendors, workers, and owners may capture different shares of the benefits.

## 6. Core strategic principles

1. **Earn trust through fulfilled responsibilities.** A clear promise and reliable delivery should explain why someone recommends the Osiers.
2. **Build resources that survive a business-model change.** Family continuity should not depend entirely on one service, employer, platform, or founder.
3. **Distinguish belonging, employment, management, and ownership.** Children should have meaningful opportunities without compulsory participation or automatic management entitlement.
4. **Accumulate transferable capability.** Look for trained people, documented methods, customer relationships, and productive assets that can endure beyond Jordan's direct labor.
5. **Preserve multiple objectives.** Evaluate enterprise viability, family resilience, community benefit, and institutional development separately.
6. **Learn broadly; commit on evidence.** Comprehensive public research is appropriate. Increasing capital and operating commitments requires stronger evidence.
7. **Use local rootedness with an open economic boundary.** Outside revenue, regional partnerships, and national markets may support local stewardship.
8. **Strengthen existing institutions where that works best.** New organizations are one possible means of contribution.
9. **Keep service and commercial relationships clear.** Church and community participation should remain comfortable for people who never become customers. Keep confidential client knowledge separate from family investment research.
10. **Keep family capacity explicit.** Health, relationships, parenting, and voluntary participation are constraints and sources of strength, not free inputs.

## 7. First-principles research program

### A. County wealth and dependency model

Study how earnings, spending, investment, ownership, goods, services, and talent enter, circulate within, and leave the county. Identify important external dependencies and local productive capabilities.

Decision enabled: whether to serve existing local demand, bring outside revenue into the county, improve established enterprises, or help preserve important local capacity.

### B. Organization of work

Map actors, responsibilities, handoffs, verification, exceptions, and purchasing authority. Investigate why work is performed internally, outsourced, shared, or left to households. Examine whether technology changes the economics of those arrangements.

Decision enabled: which bounded responsibilities an enterprise could own more effectively than available alternatives.

### C. Value creation and retention under AI

For each candidate, identify what becomes cheaper, who benefits, how competitors respond, and what remains valuable. Examine the possibility that buyers or existing software vendors absorb the service directly.

Decision enabled: whether a proposed advantage can produce sustainable earnings and transferable assets.

### D. Trust and cooperation

Study documented partnerships, purchasing relationships, referrals, and the people or institutions that coordinate activity. Distinguish public affiliations from evidence of trust or influence.

Decision enabled: where to learn, collaborate, and establish a reputation through useful work.

### E. Family resources and sequencing

Track financial resources, operating capability, relationships, reputation, and family capacity. Examine what each strategic move consumes and creates.

Decision enabled: an achievable sequence of experiments, operating responsibility, delegation, ownership, and contribution.

### F. Governance and succession

Establish proportionate decision rights, risk limits, participation expectations, leadership standards, and continuity arrangements. More elaborate governance should follow actual complexity.

Decision enabled: how the enterprise can remain healthy across absence, disagreement, changing interests, and generations.

### G. Talent development and community contribution

Investigate routes into useful work, supervised responsibility, leadership, partnership, cultural participation, and spiritual life. Distinguish a commercially supported activity from a deliberate use of surplus for public benefit.

Decision enabled: what the institution could make possible for others as its capacity grows.

## 8. County research scope

The broad scan should cover all major industry sectors, employer and nonemployer activity, public functions, nonprofits, congregations and community organizations, household needs, and regional providers serving the county.

Use a coverage ledger rather than claiming literal completeness. Record observed presence, unresolved coverage, source limitations, and exclusions. Study both internal workflows and interorganizational handoffs. Preserve physical work, infrastructure, care, and industrial activity within scope.

The companion digital-model brief specifies the public-data system supporting this research. Its role is to organize evidence, expose uncertainty, identify questions, and compare choices. It cannot establish private willingness to pay or spiritual health through public proxies alone.

## 9. Institutional options to preserve

| Possible vehicle | Mechanism to investigate | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owned operating company | Repeated delivery builds customers, capability, and earnings | Can it operate reliably within family capacity? |
| Specialized research or professional service | Judgment and expertise solve consequential decisions | Does it produce repeat demand beyond founder labor? |
| Operating partnership | Systems capability combines with an owner's customers and expertise | Incentives, authority, and partner quality |
| Acquisition | Existing demand and operations support ownership | True earnings, management requirements, and downside exposure |
| Software or data product | Repeated workflows become scalable tools | Distribution, retention, competition, and maintenance economics |
| Group of related enterprises | Shared capabilities improve several businesses | Whether benefits exceed coordination costs |
| Training and employment institution | Developing people strengthens delivery and community capacity | Funding, supervision, and routes to real responsibility |
| Broader-market business with local reinvestment | External earnings support rooted ownership and contribution | Whether local purpose survives competing demands |

These may combine over time. No sequence is predetermined.

## 10. Opportunity assessment and evidence gates

Evaluate candidates using a transparent comparison rather than an unexplained composite score.

| Dimension | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer and problem | Who decides, who pays, what triggers demand, and what is the consequence of failure? |
| Alternatives | How is the work handled today, and what would motivate switching? |
| Economics | What remains after delivery labor, sales effort, review, maintenance, overhead, and a realistic cost for founder labor? |
| Repeatability | Does the responsibility recur, and can the process be taught? |
| AI durability | What happens as competitors and customers gain better tools? |
| Family compatibility | What availability, capital, and operating exposure are required? |
| Local contribution | What useful capability, access, affordability, or opportunity improves? |
| Transferable ownership | What exists after an engagement beyond payment for time? |
| Evidence | Which claims are observed, inferred, hypothesized, or scenario-dependent? |

Evidence should progress from local presence to observed workflow, demonstrated problem, identified purchasing authority, paid test, repeat purchase, and repeatable delivery. Earlier evidence does not imply later evidence.

Before expanding, specify what would disprove the thesis. Examples include no willingness to pay, adequate existing alternatives, constant founder intervention, inaccessible purchasing decisions, or economics that fail after full labor costs.

## 11. Time horizons and review

- **Near term:** establish the household baseline; build the public research foundation; compare theses; test a limited number of concrete offers.
- **Around the March 2027 birth:** design obligations for reduced availability. Exact capacity and coverage must be established with the family.
- **Remainder of the three-year horizon:** pursue repeat demand, sound economics, documented delivery, shared responsibility, and appropriate ownership as evidence supports them.
- **Generational horizon:** preserve resources, freedom, governance, talent development, and the ability to support worthwhile institutions.

A proposed review rhythm is quarterly and after major family, employment, customer, or market changes. Review evidence, assumptions, actual hours, economics, and family effects. A new model release alone does not determine strategy.

## 12. Commercialization of the county model

The model may generate direct research revenue, recurring monitoring work, decision-support engagements, or eventual software access. It may also improve the family's own decisions and relationships.

Track those benefits separately. Marketability remains unvalidated. Existing products such as Esri Business Analyst and Lightcast demonstrate adjacent offerings, not demand for this specific model. A promising initial test is one buyer group, one recurring decision, and one paid deliverable produced from a small part of the model.

Broad strategic scope should remain available even if a narrow paid application funds part of the work. Client priorities must not silently rewrite the county's factual representation.

## 13. Guidance for future sessions

1. State which decision the session is intended to improve.
2. Preserve the established mission and distinguish proposals from commitments.
3. Use public evidence for researchable questions; ask the family only for genuinely personal constraints or choices.
4. Refresh time-sensitive claims and attach sources, dates, geography, and limitations.
5. Use parallel research agents when helpful; Jordan has expressed a preference for the “6 Sol” model when available. Assign distinct coverage and a shared evidence schema.
6. Produce a concrete output: a source audit, model component, comparative thesis, interview brief, experiment, or decision memo.
7. Record what changed, what remains uncertain, and the next decision enabled.
8. Research authorization does not authorize contacting people, publishing, spending money, or making commitments on the family's behalf.

Suggested opening instruction for a new strategy session:

> Use this framework as context. Identify the most consequential unresolved strategic question we can advance with available evidence. Present alternative theses, examine counterevidence, and recommend a concrete next research or validation step. Keep the family's generational purpose distinct from assumptions about the first business.

## 14. Selected reference starting points

These references informed the discussion or provide starting points for later research. They are not proof of local demand. Recheck current sources before relying on them for decisions.

- [METR task-completion time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/) — capability evidence with explicit task and reliability limits.
- [Census NAICS structure](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/year/2022/guidance/understanding-naics.html) — systematic industry coverage.
- [Chesterfield financial transparency](https://www.chesterfield.gov/financialtransparency) — public financial and planning sources.
- [Esri Business Analyst](https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/arcgis-business-analyst/overview) and [Lightcast economic development](https://lightcast.io/use-cases/economic-development) — adjacent commercial offerings.
