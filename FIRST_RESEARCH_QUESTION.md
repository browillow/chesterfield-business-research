## Question and decision

**Which recurring outsourced service responsibilities in Chesterfield County and
its public schools show documented purchasing activity and deserve deeper
investigation as a family-enterprise opportunity?**

Select two or three contrasting responsibilities from observed records, including
physical or operational work if the evidence supports it. Do not preselect a
software category. The decision is **advance, hold or reject further investigation**
for each candidate, not which business to start or which opportunities are best
countywide.

Observable purchasing is a good first pipeline exercise: named buyers, dated
records and distinguishable transactions allow a modest, auditable comparison.
It can connect a source to provenance, entities/relations, a calculation and a
finding without first modeling the county. Its procurement bias is substantial:
it favors visible public spending and established tender categories, omits much
internal work and private demand, and may miss small purchases, subcontracting
and opportunities that never become tenders. Purchasing does not establish an
unmet problem, switching willingness, accessible sales or profitable delivery.

## Responsibilities, competing explanations and comparison

Choose candidates only after the feasibility pass shows purchasing activity.
Contrast materially different delivery demands. For each, identify the documented
buyer, payer and purchasing channel separately from the operational user or inferred
decision maker. Describe workflow, trigger, failure consequence, alternatives, repeat mechanism
and documented switching reasons.

Competing explanations: recurrent outsourcing supports a transferable operating
capability; incumbents already provide an adequate economical service; apparent
recurrence is one long contract, duplicate notices or exceptional work; procurement
requirements make entry impractical. Seek strongest counterevidence: incumbent
tenure, bundled scope, qualification,
insurance, staffing, bonding, equipment, response-time or renewal constraints.

Compare observed award values and terms only where comparable. Record unknown
labor, overhead, sales effort, capital and profit as unknown; any delivery-economic
scenario must expose assumptions and include founder labor. No private demand or
profit estimate may be inferred from public spend. Consider business vehicles only
where relevant. Describe family demands such as daytime supervision, emergency
coverage and delegation. Hours, capital, downside limits and family participation remain unknown; ask
personal questions only when material to the next decision.

exact URL, access date, coverage/terms; artifact ID/hash and original locator;
retrieval event/method and observed metadata; evidence ID with page/row/field,
excerpt, period, units and limitations. Separate extracts from originals.

Create only necessary buyer, supplier, responsibility and award identities.
Proposed directed relations are `buyer awards contract to supplier`, `contract
covers responsibility` and `award effective during period`, each linked to evidence.
Keep source-native identifiers and ambiguous entity matches unresolved. Assertions
carry `reported`, `calculated`, `inference`, `hypothesis` or `scenario` labels and
separate proposed/accepted review status. Findings cite supported observations or
derivations with their scope; opportunity hypotheses have a separate disposition.

The default reproducible calculation is **observed awarded service-year coverage**:
for each responsibility, count distinct selected fiscal years with at least one
verified award; show the inspected years as denominator. Also report unique award
IDs per year if records permit deduplication. Attribute by documented award date
under the verified fiscal calendar; ongoing contract coverage is a separate
measure. A multi-year contract may involve repeated service despite just one
award; this calculation measures observed awarding, not annual demand. Pin selected
evidence, classification mapping, deduplication rules and
Python/SQL query. Never extrapolate sample counts to county totals or combine
award ceilings with payments. Repeated payments do not prove recurring contracts.
If awards or comparable
periods are unavailable, report coverage and omit recurrence calculations;
missing procurement data does not establish absent demand.
